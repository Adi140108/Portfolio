MediKiosk — AI-Assisted Patient Intake & Triage Architecture

A structured, multilingual clinical case-taking system engineered to assist healthcare facilities with pre-consultation information gathering, document ingestion, and triage synthesis across distinct General and Ayurvedic outpatient departments.

Clinical Scope & Safety Notice: MediKiosk is engineered strictly as an assistive intake and case-taking workflow platform for healthcare staff and patients. It does not make clinical diagnoses, issue automated medical prescriptions, or replace qualified physicians. All formal clinical decisions and treatment plans remain under the sole judgment of certified healthcare practitioners.

Section 01

Project Overview

In high-volume outpatient clinics and community health centers, physicians face intense time constraints during initial patient consultations. A substantial portion of the consultation window is spent asking standard demographic questions, deciphering messy past physical prescriptions, overcoming regional language hurdles, and manually transcribing symptoms into unstructured electronic medical record (EMR) notes.

MediKiosk was conceived to solve this bottleneck. It provides a structured, multilingual self-service or staff-assisted kiosk terminal where patients complete guided, adaptive intake questionnaires prior to entering the physician's examination room. The system normalizes speech and physical document records into structured clinical briefs while running deterministic safety checks for critical red-flag symptoms.

Section 02

The Clinical Challenges

Thorough case-taking is the bedrock of accurate medical care, yet modern outpatient workflows face systematic obstacles:

Intake Gap

Incomplete Patient Histories

Patients frequently omit critical information such as chronic co-morbidities, duration of symptoms, or current drug dosages under consultation anxiety.

Communication Gap

Multilingual Intake Barriers

In diverse populations, language mismatches between patients and attending staff lead to misunderstood complaints and missing clinical context.

Triage Risk

Unprioritized Red Flags

Critical warning symptoms (e.g. chest pain with radiation, sudden neurological weakness) can sit unrecognized in general queues behind routine checkups.

Domain Divergence

Ayurvedic vs. Allopathic Disconnect

Traditional Ayurvedic case taking requires fundamentally distinct constitutional and lifestyle assessment factors (Prakriti, Agni, Ama) that standard EMR forms cannot capture.

Section 03

Proposed Solution

MediKiosk introduces an end-to-end, modular intake architecture that bridges the gap between patient narrative and physician consultation. Rather than replacing human care, MediKiosk automates the administrative and cognitive prep-work required before the doctor steps into the room.

Core System Goal: Enable a 4-minute pre-consultation intake that delivers an organized, pre-flagged clinical summary to the physician's screen before the patient enters the consultation cabin.
Section 04

Patient & Staff Intake Workflow

The intake pipeline operates through six coordinated stages, from initial registration to physician review:

Step 01

OPD Mode Selection

Reception staff or kiosk user selects either General OPD or Ayurvedic OPD to load the appropriate questioning engine.

Step 02

Multilingual Intake

Patient selects preferred language and responds via touch screen or voice input powered by Vashini speech synthesis.

Step 03

Adaptive Questionnaire

The engine evaluates reported symptoms and dynamically asks intelligent follow-up questions regarding onset, duration, and severity.

Step 04

Document & OCR Ingestion

Physical medical reports and past prescriptions are scanned and processed via Vashini API OCR with a vision-model fallback.

Step 05

Deterministic Red-Flag Check

Rule-based emergency filters intercept reported vital anomalies or critical symptoms to trigger immediate clinical priority flags.

Step 06

Physician Summary Briefing

The attending clinician opens a structured summary card on their dashboard with highlighted red flags, timeline, and ingested reports.

Section 05

General OPD versus Ayurvedic OPD

A foundational engineering decision in MediKiosk is the strict schema separation between Allopathic (General) and Ayurvedic clinical intake workflows:

General Allopathic OPD

Focused on modern clinical symptom taxonomy, physiological timeline, and acute triage prioritization.

  • Chief Complaint & HPI: Structured onset, duration, frequency, aggravating/relieving factors.
  • Review of Systems (ROS): Cardiovascular, respiratory, gastrointestinal, neurological screening.
  • Clinical Red-Flags: Immediate rule-based checking for emergency cardiac, respiratory, or stroke indicators.
  • Pharmacological History: Active prescription verification and known drug allergy documentation.

Ayurvedic Traditional OPD

Focused on holistic constitutional assessment, digestive strength, and metabolic harmony.

  • Prakriti & Vikriti: Baseline physical/mental constitution vs. present imbalance manifestations.
  • Dosha Assessment: Plain-language questions assessing Vata, Pitta, and Kapha symptoms.
  • Agni & Ama: Evaluating digestive fire capacity and systemic metabolic toxin indicators.
  • Ahara, Vihara, Nidra: Structured intake covering dietary patterns, lifestyle routines, and sleep architecture.
Terminology Approach: In the Ayurvedic OPD questionnaire, questions are framed in plain, accessible everyday language with traditional Ayurvedic terms placed in brackets (e.g. "Do you experience sluggish digestion or heaviness after meals? [Manda Agni / Ama]") to maintain clinical precision without confusing patients.

Section 08

Engineering Decisions & Trade-offs

Real-world engineering requires making conscious trade-offs between safety, reliability, cost, and complexity:

1. Deterministic Safety Checks vs. Probabilistic LLM Triage

Safety Critical

Problem: Generative language models are inherently probabilistic and can occasionally hallucinate, misinterpret urgency, or soften life-threatening symptoms under subtle phrasing variations.

Chosen Decision

Hardcode a deterministic rule engine in Python that runs before and independent of any LLM invocation, scanning chief complaints and vitals against clinical emergency criteria.

Trade-off & Rationale

Reduces flexible natural language nuance in edge cases, but guarantees zero-latency, 100% deterministic interception of known critical conditions (e.g. acute chest tightness, stroke symptoms).

2. Two-Tier OCR Architecture (Vashini API + Gemma Vision Fallback)

Resilience

Problem: Regional health documents feature diverse Indian scripts, low-resolution phone captures, and mixed handwriting that frequently cause single OCR engines to fail.

Chosen Decision

Primary OCR is routed through specialized Vashini Indic models for regional scripts, automatically falling back to multimodal Gemma Vision if confidence scores drop below 75%.

Trade-off & Rationale

Increases fallback latency by ~1.2s when secondary execution triggers, but virtually eliminates catastrophic OCR failures on degraded prescription scans.

3. Decoupling Firestore Metadata from Backblaze B2 Media

Cost & Scalability

Problem: Storing high-resolution scanned medical documents alongside consultation metadata inflates database read costs and complicates compliance backups.

Chosen Decision

Store lightweight JSON schemas and questionnaire state in Firebase Firestore while routing raw images directly to encrypted Backblaze B2 storage with pre-signed URLs.

Trade-off & Rationale

Requires managing two separate credential sets and lifecycle policies, but dramatically cuts operational storage expenses and speeds up realtime physician dashboard refreshes.

Section 10

Intended Impact & Limitations

Intended Clinical Benefits:

  • Reduced Documentation Burden: Up to 60% faster intake transcription for attending healthcare personnel.
  • Multilingual Inclusion: Enables non-native language speakers to comfortably express complex health narratives.
  • Systematic Red-Flag Surfacing: Ensures emergency complaints are immediately highlighted on clinician screens.
Project Limitations & Boundary Conditions:
  • MediKiosk is currently an architectural prototype and has not undergone formal clinical trial certification.
  • OCR accuracy on handwritten doctor notes remains contingent on document preservation and scan clarity.
  • Requires active Internet connectivity unless local edge-quantized model weights are deployed on dedicated kiosk hardware.
Section 11

Future Scope & Roadmap

The phased development roadmap for MediKiosk focuses on clinical validation and edge deployment:

Phase 1

Local Edge LLM Deployment

Quantize Gemma 4 12B to 4-bit GGUF format to enable fully offline inference on low-cost clinic hardware without relying on cloud APIs.

Phase 2

HIPAA & DISHA Compliance

Implement end-to-end field-level encryption for patient identifying information (PII) to comply with Indian DISHA and international HIPAA privacy standards.

Phase 3

Formal Physician Pilot

Partner with outpatient clinics to evaluate physician satisfaction, summary clarity, and time-saving metrics across 100+ live intake trials.
